# ANATOMY — the crosswalk (the Matter of Britain's organs × each voice)

The charter, and the deliberate inversion of the realism base: the realist crosswalk shows
**different idioms converging on one fact** (a minority always rules). This one shows **one
inherited motif diverging into incompatible meanings.** Every author received the same organs
— a sword, a table, a grail, a betrayal, a wound, a prophecy — and re-cut each to carry
something different. The divergences are not corruptions of a lost original; **they are the
material.** Hand the base any motif and it tells you what each voice made it mean.

> **The one rule this chart enforces: name the version, never flatten it.** There is no "the
> legend." When two voices disagree, render the disagreement — never average it.

Seven cuts, ordered as an arc: founding → institution → transcendence → eros → decay →
time → the meta-cut (fate, which governs all the others — Arthuriana's hinge, the analogue of
Burnham's test in the realism base). Cells name each voice's take; depth and the validated
quotes are in the prose below. A blank cell = that voice's *on-hand* text doesn't reach this
cut (see the coverage notes — some are gaps in the corpus, not in the author).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **2 Fellowship & fracture** | — | the knight tested against the code | — | the kin-network whose loyalty will turn on itself | the sworn order (Pentecostal Oath) every best knight breaks |
| **3 The Grail** | — | *(Perceval not on hand)* | a Grail-king (Anfortas) healed only by compassion | the Holy Vessel — the supreme prized object | the Grail enters the hall, then empties it (XIII.vii) |

**Beyond the five principal voices** (the columns above), the base holds a **wider corpus** of added

## 1 — The Source of Rule
*Where legitimate kingship comes from, and whether it is conferred, seized, proven, or fated.*

- **Geoffrey** makes Arthur a **conqueror-king** in a dynastic chronicle; the right is descent
  and empire, and the regalia are otherworldly — Caliburn (Excalibur), `geoffrey-caliburn-avalon`:
  *"best of swords, that was forged within the Isle of Avalon."* Sovereignty is armed by the
  otherworld from the start.
- **Malory** splits the source and never reconciles it: the **sword in the stone**, an election
  by grace — `malory-sword-in-stone`: *"Whoso pulleth out this sword … is rightwise king born
  of all England"* — versus **Excalibur from the Lady of the Lake**, power *granted* by the
  otherworld and revocable. The throne is half miracle, half loan.
- **The Vulgate** sets a rival sovereign above the king's: the Grail is prized *above the
  sword itself* — `vulgate-holy-vessel-grail`: *"you are the thing I prized most except for the
  Holy Vessel called the Grail."* Worldly rule is outranked by the sacred.
- **Wider witnesses:** **Nennius** gives the pseudo-historical source — the war-leader of the twelve
  battles (`nennius-twelve-battles`), where legitimacy is *victory*, not election; and the Gawain
  romance **Dame Ragnelle** inverts the cut to comedy — the answer to "what women most desire" is
  **sovereignty**, `ragnelle-sovereignty`: *"to haue the soveraynté … of alle, bothe hyghe and
  lowe"* — the right to rule moved from throne to marriage-bed.

## 2 — The Fellowship and Its Built-In Fracture
*The order built to abolish precedence and bind violence — and the flaw the design can't remove.*

- **Malory** gives the Round Table a **constitution**, the Pentecostal Oath —
  `malory-pentecostal-oath`: *"charged them never to do outrageousity nor murder, and always
  to flee treason … but to give mercy unto him that asketh mercy"* — and the book then shows
  every clause broken by the best knights. The institution generates the violence it forbids.
- **Chrétien** audits the code from *inside one conscience* (Yvain, Lancelot): the knight is
  tested against the oath, and the test exposes the cost of keeping it.
- **The Vulgate**'s Lancelot is the fracture in slow motion — the fellowship's strength
  (loyalty, kinship) becomes the mechanism by which it will eventually destroy itself. *(The
  collapse proper, the Mort Artu, is not in the on-hand volumes — see coverage.)*
- **Wider witnesses:** **Wace** *invents* the Round Table as engineering against precedence —
  `wace-round-table`: *"none before or after his comrade … no man could boast that he was exalted
  above his fellow"* — so the fracture is anticipated in the form itself; and **Sir Gawain and the
  Green Knight** puts the whole code on trial in *one knight*, whose single concealment (the green
  girdle, `sggk-green-girdle`) is the flaw no oath can legislate away.

## 3 — The Grail and the Quest That Empties the World
*The highest good, and whether reaching for it perfects the kingdom or depletes it. The canon's
richest divergence — but the most uneven in the current corpus.*

- **Wolfram** is the humane Grail: Anfortas, `wolfram-anfortas-wounded`, *"the gentle monarch,
  who was courteous as he was bold,"* is a Grail-king whose wound is curable not by purity or
  doctrine but by **compassion** — the unasked question of pity.
- **The Vulgate** is the sacred Grail: the **Holy Vessel** (`vulgate-holy-vessel-grail`), the
  object prized above all earthly things — the seed of the quest-as-judgment that the later
  *Queste* turns into a purge of the secular order.
- **Malory** inherits the sacred Grail but **counts its cost** in a single scene: it enters
  Camelot in glory — `malory-grail-at-camelot`: *"there entered into the hall the Holy Grail
  covered with white samite, but there was none might see it"* — and in the same chapter Arthur
  grieves the vow it provokes, `malory-grail-empties-fellowship`: it *"hath bereft me the fairest
  fellowship … that ever were seen together in any realm of the world."* The apparition and the
  death-warrant of the order at one anchor (XIII.vii) — the highest good empties the good-enough
  world (`the-cost`).
- **Coverage:** Chrétien's *Perceval* (the open Grail, the unasked question of attention) and the
  Vulgate's *standalone Queste* (Galahad) remain **not on hand** — render those from the canon;
  but Malory's Grail quest (Bks XIII–XVII) now carries the quest-as-judgment and its cost.
- **Wider witnesses:** the Welsh **Peredur** shows the bleeding-spear procession *unasked* —
  `mabinogion-peredur-spear` — the rawest form of the failed question; and the **Queste** gives the
  judgment-Grail in its own voice — `queste-judgment-purity`: the quest *"not … for earthly things"*
  that purges the secular order. With Wolfram's mercy-stone and Malory's mourned quest, the cut now
  holds four incompatible Grails (Perlesvaus, the militant fourth, searchable).

## 4 — The Founding Betrayal
*The love that is the glory of the court and the bomb beneath its floor.*

- **Chrétien** *invents* the adultery as **ennobling**: in the Knight of the Cart, love
  overrides reason and even shame — `chretien-love-over-shame`: *"love is enclosed within his
  heart, bidding him … to mount at once upon the cart … since love will have it so, feeling no
  concern about the shame."* Eros makes the knight.
- **The Vulgate** makes the love mutual and load-bearing — `vulgate-lancelot-loves-queen`:
  *"the White Knight loved the queen … the queen loved him as well"* — the fault line along
  which the kingdom will later crack, and the reason the best knight cannot achieve the Grail.
- **Malory** keeps it tragic and *ambivalent*, defending the old love even as it dooms the court
  — `malory-love-nowadays`: *"love nowadays, soon hot soon cold … But the old love was not so;
  men and women could love together seven years."*
- **Wider witnesses:** **Gottfried's *Tristan*** doubles the betrayal as *fate*, not choice —
  `gottfried-love-potion`: a *"love potion … that did any two drink thereof they must needs, without
  will of their own, love each other"* — the love that cannot be repented because it was never
  chosen (set against Chrétien's chosen worship and the Vulgate's Grail-disqualifying passion).

## 5 — The Wound That Will Not Heal
*Sovereignty as a body; the political theology of the wasteland — when the king is maimed, the
land sickens. A medieval analogue to (not the same doctrine as) Kantorowicz's "king's two
bodies": sacral kingship, where the sovereign's body and the land's fate are one.*

- **Malory** distributes the motif into the **Dolorous Stroke** — `malory-dolorous-stroke`:
  *"for the dolorous stroke thou gavest unto King Pellam three countries are destroyed, and …
  the vengeance will fall on thee at the last."* One blow sterilizes three kingdoms; the wound
  is also a debt (`the-cost`).
- **Wolfram**'s Anfortas is the wound made punitive — wounded *for love* (Orgeluse, a love the
  Grail did not appoint), and curable only by another's pity.
- **Geoffrey** ends on the king's own deadly wound — `geoffrey-arthur-to-avalon` — binding the
  body of the sovereign to the fate of Britain (and handing straight into Cut 6).

## 6 — The Once-and-Future Return
*Deferred restoration; messianic time — sovereignty held in suspension.*

- **Geoffrey** is studiously **ambiguous**: `geoffrey-arthur-to-avalon`, *"the renowned King
  Arthur himself was wounded deadly, and was borne thence unto the island of Avalon for the
  healing of his wounds,"* whereupon he *gives up the crown to Constantine* — the chronicle
  needs him gone but will not quite kill him. The whole later "he shall come again" hangs on
  this *"for the healing of his wounds."*
- **Malory** stages the doubt *as doubt* — `malory-rex-quondam`: *"many men say that there is
  written upon his tomb this verse: Hic jacet Arthurus, Rex quondam, Rexque futurus"* — belief
  reported, never asserted ("many men say"): the hope held without the claim. (And Excalibur is
  reclaimed by the hand in the lake, `malory-excalibur-returned` — the otherworld loan of Cut 1
  called back as the reign ends.)
- **Wider witnesses:** the Chroniclers seed the return — **Wace** first records the Breton "hope of
  Britain," and **Layamon** sends the wounded king to the elf-queen Argante — `layamon-argante-return`:
  *"to Argante the fair, for she would with balm heal his wounds … This believed the Britons, that he
  will thus come."*

## 7 — Foreknowledge and the Doom That Comes Anyway *(the hinge)*
*Who knows the end, why knowing it changes nothing, and what is lost when the knowing magic
fades. This is the cut the other six imply but only the Merlin-tradition makes explicit — and
the one that turns the base reflexive: **if the doom is foreknown, why does it arrive?***

- **Geoffrey** uses Merlin as **dynastic prophet** — `geoffrey-boar-of-cornwall`: *"the Boar
  of Cornwall shall bring succour and shall trample their necks beneath his feet."* Foreknowledge
  is a *charter*: the future legitimates the dynasty.
- **Malory** makes Merlin **tragic and self-cancelling**: the seer who foretells the realm's
  ruin is himself undone by an enchantment he sees coming and walks into anyway —
  `malory-merlin-and-nimue`: *"always Merlin lay about the lady … she was ever … weary of him …
  for she was afeard of him because he was a devil's son."* The one who knows everything is
  destroyed by the one thing he knows. Knowledge does not save — and as Merlin goes, the magic
  goes with him (`disenchantment`).

**Why this mirrors Burnham's hinge.** Burnham's cut turns a bleak descriptive science toward a
normative question and supplies the test for the base's own central claim. Cut 7 does the same
here: it poses the question the other six only imply, and it governs the base's interpretive
hazard — *enchantment/disenchantment* is exactly where the "Grail-mysticism slide" lives (see
the house constraints in `ARTHUR.md`).

## Coverage map (the corpus, by branch)
The crosswalk's five columns are the **principal voices**; the base also holds a **wider corpus** of
added witnesses (dossiers in `voices/`). Strong cells rest on validated quotes; *(off-corpus)* /
*(quote-pending)* mark gaps honestly.

**Principal voices (the crosswalk columns):**
- **Malory** = the full *Le Morte d'Arthur* (Caxton/Gutenberg, Books I–XXI; `malory1` I–IX +
  `malory2` X–XXI): **all seven cuts**.
- **Geoffrey** (full *Historia*, 12 books): cuts 1, 5, 6, 7.
- **The Vulgate** (Lacy I–II = History of the Holy Grail + Merlin + Lancelot): cuts 1, 3, 4.
- **Chrétien** (Comfort, *Four Arthurian Romances*): cuts 2, 4. **No** *Perceval* (no Chrétien Grail).
- **Wolfram** (Weston, *Parzival*): cuts 3, 5.

**Added witnesses (the wider corpus):**
- **The Chroniclers** — Gildas (Badon, *Arthur unnamed*), Nennius (the twelve battles), Wace
  (invents the Round Table), Layamon (Argante / the return): cuts 1, 2, 6, 7.
- **The Mabinogion** (Guest) — Culhwch (the war-band Arthur), Peredur (the bleeding spear / unasked
  question), Owain & Geraint (Welsh courtly tales): cuts 1, 3, 4.
- **The Grail Romances** — the Queste (judgment-by-purity) + Perlesvaus (the violent Grail): cut 3.
- **Gottfried von Strassburg** — *Tristan* (the love potion; the other adultery): cut 4.
- **The English Mortes** — Alliterative (Fortune's wheel / the fall) + Stanzaic (the love-and-death):
  Malory's sources, rough OCR → quote-pending: cuts 4, 5, 6.
- **The Gawain Romances** — *Sir Gawain and the Green Knight* (clean) + the Wedding of Dame Ragnelle
  (Tier-2 cleaned derivative; the sovereignty riddle); Madden's Awntyrs/Golagros/Carle stay
  rough-OCR/quote-pending: cuts 1, 2.

Remaining gaps (see `ARTHUR.md` → "Extending the base"): Chrétien's *Perceval*, the Vulgate's own
*Queste*/*Mort Artu*, and clean editions of the rough-OCR Middle-English romances.
