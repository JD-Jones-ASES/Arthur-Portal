# The Chroniclers (6th–13th c.) — the pseudo-historical spine

Gildas, Nennius, Wace, Layamon — the line that built (and quietly questioned) Arthur *as
history* before the romancers gave him an interior. (Geoffrey of Monmouth, the hinge of this
line, keeps his own dossier; here are the witnesses on either side of him.)

## The apparatus
- **Gildas** (*De Excidio*, c. 540) — the one near-contemporary witness — names the British
  victory at **Mount Badon** and pointedly **does not name Arthur**. The "historical" Arthur is
  absent from the only source close to the events. *(In the corpus via the Six Old English
  Chronicles; searchable, quote-pending.)*
- **Nennius** (*Historia Brittonum*, c. 830) supplies the first Arthur — the **twelve battles**:
  `nennius-twelve-battles` [Nennius §56]: *"The twelfth was a most severe contest, when Arthur
  penetrated to the hill of Badon. In this engagement, nine hundred and forty fell by his hand
  alone, no one but the Lord affording him assistance."* A war-leader (*dux bellorum*), not yet a king.
- **Wace** (*Roman de Brut*, 1155) renders Geoffrey into French and **invents the Round Table** as
  a device against precedence — `wace-round-table` [Wace, Roman de Brut]: *"none before or after
  his comrade … no man could boast that he was exalted above his fellow, for all alike were
  gathered round the board."* He also first records the Breton **"hope of Britain"** (the return).
- **Layamon** (*Brut*, c. 1200) Englishes Wace and deepens the marvellous: the elf-queen
  **Argante** bears the wounded king to Avalon — `layamon-argante-return` [Layamon, Brut]: *"to
  Argante the fair, for she would with balm heal his wounds … This believed the Britons, that he
  will thus come."*

## Steelman
The chroniclers turn a possible sixth-century war-leader into a *usable* past — and Wace and
Layamon hand the romancers two load-bearing devices (the Round Table; the deferred return) before
a single courtly romance is written.

## The standard objection
This is the seam where fiction is sold as history, and **Gildas's silence is the rebuke**: the
more confidently a chronicler names Arthur, the further he stands from the events. The base studies
the *manufacture* of the historical Arthur; it never joins the "real King Arthur" hunt (the
nationalist pseudo-history slide).

## How it maps
The root of **Cut 1** (legitimacy as descent/conquest), **Cut 6** (the return — Wace's "hope,"
Layamon's Argante), and **Cut 7** (foreknowledge — Nennius's battle-roll, Geoffrey's prophecies).
Gildas/Nennius precede Geoffrey; Wace and Layamon carry him into the vernaculars and toward romance.
