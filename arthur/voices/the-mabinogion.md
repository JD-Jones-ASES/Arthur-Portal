# The Mabinogion (Welsh; compiled 12th–14th c.) — the Celtic root

The Welsh tales (Lady Charlotte Guest's public-domain translation) that preserve the oldest and
strangest Arthur — the chief of a war-band of marvels — and Welsh analogues to Chrétien's
romances that run *beneath* the French tradition.

## The apparatus
- **Culhwch and Olwen** — the earliest Welsh Arthur: not an emperor but the chief whose warriors
  accomplish a string of impossible tasks; Arthur as the hub of a heroic war-band. *(Searchable;
  the Welsh substratum of Cut 1.)*
- **Peredur** — the Welsh **Perceval**, with the canon's eeriest Grail-procession analogue: a
  severed head on a salver and the **bleeding spear** — `mabinogion-peredur-spear` [Peredur]:
  *"bearing a spear of mighty size, with three streams of blood flowing from the point to the
  ground. And when all the company saw this, they began wailing and lamenting."* Peredur, like
  Perceval, **does not ask** — the unasked question in its rawest form.
- **Owain (The Lady of the Fountain)** and **Geraint** — Welsh tellings of Chrétien's *Yvain* and
  *Erec*: the courtly trials of the knight, on the Welsh side of the Channel. *(Searchable.)*

## Steelman
The Mabinogion is the evidence that the Matter of Britain is **British before it is French** — the
motifs the romancers seem to invent have Celtic roots. Peredur shows the unasked question and the
bleeding spear in a form older and stranger than Chrétien's.

## The standard objection
Dating is treacherous: the manuscripts are thirteenth–fourteenth century, and how much is
pre-Chrétien tradition versus Welsh borrowing *back* from the French is genuinely contested. Don't
overstate priority — render the parallel, flag the uncertainty (a `version-divergence` node).

## How it maps
The Celtic root beneath **Cut 1** (Culhwch's war-band Arthur), **Cut 3** (Peredur's bleeding spear
and unasked question — the Welsh face of the Grail), and **Cut 4** (Owain/Geraint, the Welsh
courtly tales). Reads under and against Chrétien.
