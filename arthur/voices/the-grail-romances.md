# The Grail Romances — Perlesvaus & the Queste

The two French Grail romances on hand beyond the Vulgate's *Lancelot* — the **judging** Grail (the
*Queste del Saint Graal*, Comfort's translation) and the **violent** Grail (Perlesvaus, Evans's
*High History of the Holy Graal*) — which carry the quest past where our Vulgate volumes stop.

## The apparatus
- **The *Queste del Saint Graal*** — the Grail as **judgment by purity**: only the cleansed may
  approach, and the quest is a purge that sorts the saved from the damned — `queste-judgment-purity`
  [Quest of the Holy Grail]: *"no one ought to enter upon such a lofty service before being cleansed
  and purged of all villainy and mortal sins. This Quest is not a quest for earthly things, but is
  to be the search for the deep secrets and confidences of Our Lord."* The take our Vulgate volumes
  only *seed* (and Malory *mourns*) here speaks in its own voice.
- **Perlesvaus / *The High History of the Holy Graal*** — the strangest, most violent Grail
  romance: a Perceval who avenges, a Grail bound up with bloodshed and "the changing of the Law."
  The Grail as conquest rather than mercy. *(Searchable; quote-pending.)*

## Steelman
Together they complete the Grail's range. With Wolfram's compassion-stone and Malory's mourned
quest, the base now holds **four incompatible Grails** — Chrétien's open question (off-corpus,
reached via Peredur), Wolfram's mercy, the Queste's judgment, Perlesvaus's militancy — the cut's
signature divergence, fully witnessed.

## The standard objection
The Queste's purity-logic reads, to many, as cruelty: the secular order — fellowship, love, the
world — is sacrificed so that one virgin may see God. Perlesvaus's holy violence sits uneasily with
"holy" at all. Grant each its own metaphysics; soften neither.

## How it maps
The hard centre of **Cut 3** (the Grail). The Queste is the judgment the Vulgate seeds and Malory
inherits into English; Perlesvaus the militant outlier. Reads against Wolfram (compassion) and the
Mabinogion's Peredur (the unasked question).
