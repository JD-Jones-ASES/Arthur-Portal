# Geoffrey of Monmouth (c. 1100–1155) — the founder

The cleric whose *History of the Kings of Britain* (c. 1136) turned a shadowy Welsh war-leader
into a **king of Britain** and handed the legend its political-historical skeleton. Everyone
after inherits his Arthur and adds the interior life he lacks. *(OCR caution: the on-hand text
is moderate quality — validate before quoting; the engine drops page-number/running-header
boilerplate and fixes a few garbles.)*

## The apparatus
- **Legitimacy as descent + conquest.** Arthur is a dynastic conqueror-emperor (the line from
  Brutus of Troy), not an elected boy. The regalia are otherworldly: Caliburn (Excalibur),
  `geoffrey-caliburn-avalon` — *"best of swords, that was forged within the Isle of Avalon."*
- **Foreknowledge as dynastic charter.** Merlin's *Prophecies* make the future legitimate the
  dynasty — `geoffrey-boar-of-cornwall`: *"the Boar of Cornwall shall bring succour and shall
  trample their necks beneath his feet."* Prophecy here is a tool of the state, not a tragedy.
- **The calculated ambiguity of the end.** Arthur is wounded deadly and *borne to Avalon "for
  the healing of his wounds,"* handing the crown to Constantine (`geoffrey-arthur-to-avalon`).
  The chronicle needs him gone but will not quite kill him — the seed of the once-and-future return.

## Steelman
Without Geoffrey there is no King Arthur — only a name in Nennius. He is the first to make the
British past a *usable* myth: a charter of legitimacy a dynasty could stand on. The whole later
tradition is a series of glosses on the figure he installed.

## The standard objection
It is fiction marketed as history — Geoffrey claimed to translate "a most ancient book" no one
else ever saw. This is where the **nationalist pseudo-history slide** begins ("the real King
Arthur"). The base's reply: it studies Geoffrey's propaganda as propaganda; it does not extend it.
