# Chrétien de Troyes (c. 1135–1190) — the inventor

The poet who created courtly Arthurian **romance** and, almost in passing, two of its load-bearing
engines: the Lancelot–Guinevere love and (in *Perceval*) the Grail. The single most generative
hand in the canon. *(On hand: Comfort's public-domain* Four Arthurian Romances *— Erec and Enide,
Cligés, Yvain, and Lancelot/the Knight of the Cart. NOT* Perceval*, so his Grail is described,
not quoted.)*

## The apparatus
- **Love as ennobling — overriding reason and shame.** In the Knight of the Cart, Lancelot's
  worship of the queen *makes* the knight: `chretien-love-over-shame` — *"love is enclosed within
  his heart, bidding him … to mount at once upon the cart … since love will have it so, feeling no
  concern about the shame."* Reason "reaches only his lips, but not his heart."
- **The knight tested against the code** (Yvain, Erec): romance as the audit of a single conscience
  against the chivalric oath — the inside view of Cut 2.
- **The open Grail** *(Perceval, off-corpus)*: the Grail introduced undefined, the failure the
  *unasked question* of attention; the romance breaks off unfinished, the wound unhealed.

## Steelman
Almost every later Arthurian motif is his seed. He made eros the engine of chivalry and invented
the form (verse romance) in which the legend would think for three centuries.

## The standard objection
He half-disowned his own creation — by his own account he wrote *Lancelot* at a patron's command
and handed off the ending. The inventor of the ennobling adultery was uneasy with it: a tension
the base should preserve, not smooth.

## How it maps
Root of **Cut 4** (the founding betrayal) and the inside-view of **Cut 2**; off-corpus, the root
of **Cut 3**. Chrétien → the Vulgate systematizes his inventions into a doom-laden cycle → Malory
inherits them through the French.
