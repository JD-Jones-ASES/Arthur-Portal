# The Vulgate / Lancelot-Grail (c. 1215–1235) — the systematizer

The vast Old French prose cycle that welded romance to theology and built a doom into its very
architecture — Malory's main source. *(On hand: Norris J. Lacy's translation, vols. I–II = the
History of the Holy Grail + the Story of Merlin + the prose Lancelot. NOT the* Queste del Saint
Graal *or the* Mort Artu*, which are later volumes. **Copyright: the Lacy translation is
in-copyright — validation-only; quote at most a short fair-use phrase and cite the translator.**)*

## The apparatus
- **The Grail as the supreme sacred object.** The Holy Vessel outranks even the sword —
  `vulgate-holy-vessel-grail`: *"you are the thing I prized most except for the Holy Vessel called
  the Grail."* The History of the Holy Grail roots the vessel in Christ's blood and Joseph of
  Arimathea — the seed the later *Queste* turns into a purge of the secular order.
- **Adultery as the load-bearing fault line.** The prose Lancelot makes the love mutual and fatal
  — `vulgate-lancelot-loves-queen`: *"the White Knight loved the queen … the queen loved him as
  well."* This is both the engine of Lancelot's greatness and the reason he cannot achieve the
  Grail: eros disqualifies from transcendence.

## Steelman
The dark synthesis behind the whole English tradition. It made the adultery *theologically*
load-bearing and engineered a cycle whose ending is encoded in its beginning — the first
Arthurian work to think like a system.

## The standard objection
Vast and repetitive, and its purity-theology (perfected later in Galahad) reads as punishing: the
secular order — the fellowship, the love, the world — is sacrificed to a sacred that only a virgin
can reach. The cost is total and, to many readers, cruel.
